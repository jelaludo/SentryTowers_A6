One concrete integration issue: your current [GLB tower builder](https://kai-denrei.github.io/spherical-stalberg-grid/src/towerlooks.js) merges the model by material and explicitly discards pivots. **Moving turrets will require preserving the articulated parts**, merging geometry only within each moving assembly.

For an initial collection, I’d propose **four families × three tiers = twelve GLBs**:

| Family                  | Tier 1 — base                                             | Tier 2 — upgrade                                                             | Tier 3 — upgrade                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Needle · sniper**     | Single long barrel, narrow angular receiver, offset scope | Longer barrel with exposed accelerator sections; enlarged rear counterweight | Exceptionally long reinforced barrel, paired longitudinal braces, substantial rear power pack |
| **Rotor · suppression** | Compact rotary barrel cluster, small ammunition drum      | Longer cluster, larger drum, reinforced rotating cradle                      | Heavy barrel shroud, twin feed drums, prominent cooling system                                |
| **Kiln · plasma**       | Two short, broad emitters around a central cooling block  | Deeper emitter housings, larger cooling fins, additional feed pipes          | Massive paired projector mouths, expanded heat exchangers, armored reservoirs                 |
| **Quiver · missiles**   | Two narrow three-cell launch pods around a central sensor | Two larger six-cell pods; strengthened elevation mounts                      | Two deep nine-cell pods, armored backs, larger central tracking sensor                        |

Keep the same base footprint, turret architecture, signature shapes, and accent placement within each family. Upgrades should remain recognizable in silhouette with materials switched off.

For the **Needle sniper**, the identity would be:

* One primary barrel at every tier.
* Narrow wedge-shaped receiver and the same offset optical sensor.
* A distinctive illuminated longitudinal channel.
* Increasing barrel reach, rear counterweight, and structural support.

Use roughly **1.0× / 1.3× / 1.6× barrel length** as a starting point. Keep the mounting center stable: a stronger version should feel like the original machine receiving heavier equipment.

For articulation, I’d standardize this node contract across the collection:

| Node        | Purpose                                   |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `ROOT`      | Placement and orientation on the planet   |
| `BASE`      | Stationary structure                      |
| `YAW`       | Turret rotation around local up           |
| `PITCH`     | Weapon elevation; child of `YAW`          |
| `RECOIL`    | Sliding weapon assembly; child of `PITCH` |
| `MUZZLE_00` | Projectile/VFX origin; follows the weapon |

Use local **+Y up**, with a documented common forward axis. This matters on your spherical board: aiming must use each tower’s local surface frame. Motors, barrels, and launch pods can use rigid transforms; these designs don’t need skeletal rigs.

I’d package each tier as a separate GLB with identical node names and compatible pivots. Keep weapon statistics in your existing gameplay definitions. Materials can use your supported unlit/emissive options; any glow halo still depends on the game’s rendering effects.
